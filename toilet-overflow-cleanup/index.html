Toilet Overflow Cleanup Help in Fort Worth

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. If it came from the tank or the supply line, it is clean. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

Our Fort Worth crew starts by identifying the water category, because that sets the rules for everything after. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Black or gray water calls for protective equipment, taking out porous materials that got soaked, carpet pad for example, and disinfecting everything it contacted. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Stop the WaterBehind the toilet is a small valve, and turning it fully clockwise shuts the water off. A stuck valve can be worked around by taking off the tank lid and lifting the float, which stops the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainDo not flush again or run sinks, showers or the washer. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterIf the water came from the bowl or contains waste, keep children and pets out, and wear gloves and waterproof shoes if you must walk through it.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathFor an upstairs toilet, look at the ceiling underneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Block the SpreadUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Contaminated water should be left to a crew that has the right disinfectants and equipment.
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Identify the Category

Before any cleaning, our crew confirms whether this is supply water or water from the drain and bowl, and sets up containment and protective measures to match.

2

Extraction and Removal

Standing water is extracted first. Contaminated drywall, baseboards or carpet pad come out where necessary, and the toilet is lifted when water has reached beneath the flange and base.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

Every fixture, the floor, the subfloor and the base of each wall gets cleaned and receives an antimicrobial treatment. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Drying and Final Readings

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. We take moisture readings until the subfloor and ceiling are back to normal levels.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • Waste water with nowhere to go because the septic tank is full or the drain field is saturated.
  • Bowl water pushed over the rim by a clog in the drain line or the trap.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Water escaping at the base with every flush because the wax ring has worn out.
  • Too much paper, wipes or a child's toy flushed and lodged in the trap.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Because disinfecting porous material is not reliable, it usually has to come out.

Subfloor Saturation

Through the grout and around the toilet base, water works its way below tile or vinyl flooring. Once wet, the plywood subfloor swells, its layers separate, and the floor covering bonded on top can come loose.

Baseboard and Door Trim Damage

Baseboards and door casings sit right at floor level and soak up water from the bottom edge. Fiberboard trim, the most common kind, swells and splits and never returns to shape.

Odor That Returns

An odor can return every time the weather turns humid if contaminated water dried beneath the floor or inside the bottom of a wall, and only removing the affected material ends it.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

A sudden and accidental overflow, for example from an unexpected clog or a fill valve that failed, is covered by many homeowners policies. Water that backs up from the sewer or a drain is often excluded unless you added a backup endorsement, and that can apply to an overflow caused by a blocked main line. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.
