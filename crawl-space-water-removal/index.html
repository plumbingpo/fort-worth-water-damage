Crawl Space Water Removal: What Fort Worth Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
  • Water beading and dripping off cold pipes or metal ducts under the floor
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Shifting Piers and Supports

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Rot You Cannot See

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Air From Below

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.
