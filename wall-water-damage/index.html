Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Fort Worth

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
  • Carpet edges or floorboards wet and discolored right next to the wall
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

The neighboring room or closet gets wet as water travels below the wall plate. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Walls You Cannot See

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.
