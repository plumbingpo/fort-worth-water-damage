Few water damage events are as common, or as potentially devastating to a property, as a pipe that bursts. At 4 to 8 gallons a minute, just one burst can send 240 to 480 gallons each hour into your ceilings, walls and flooring. The trigger may be a hard freeze, corroded or aging lines, high pressure or physical damage, but the need is the same: act immediately so water does less harm to the structure, the floors, your possessions and the building's systems.

Day or night, Fort Worth Restoration responds to burst pipe emergencies across Fort Worth and the nearby communities. After the water is out, we dry every material that took on moisture: finished floors, subflooring, wood framing, insulation and drywall. Burst pipes often damage multiple rooms and multiple levels, because water follows gravity through wall cavities, floor assemblies and ceiling spaces. We trace where the water migrated with thermal cameras and moisture detection tools, find each affected area and dry it properly, heading off the mold and structural decay that follow hidden moisture.

Burst Pipe Repair Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Know where your main water shutoff valve is and how to operate it quickly
  • Aging galvanized or polybutylene plumbing should be swapped out ahead of an unexpected failure
  • Check water pressure with a gauge, since anything over 80 PSI puts stress on fittings and pipes
  • Before winter, drain outside spigots and take the hoses off them
  • Put leak sensors by the water heater and along the main supply line
  • During freezing weather, keep faucets dripping and cabinet doors open so heat can circulate

Signs You Need Burst Pipe Repair in Fort Worth

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Exposed pipes that drip, mist or shoot water
  • Wet spots, bulging or staining on walls or ceilings
  • Rusty or discolored water coming from your taps
  • Hissing or rushing noises in the walls with every tap turned off
  • No water at a tap in freezing weather, a sign the line is frozen
  • Water pooling on floors with no apparent source
  • Water charges that jump for no clear reason, a sign of a concealed leak
